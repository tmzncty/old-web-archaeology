# Patient forums, health-status disclosure, peer support, and life-course state gaps on the Chinese old Web (2001–2015)

Status: research note, not an M1 complete reconstruction case.

Cross-repository context:

- life-history synthesis: `tmzncty/how-people-lived/topics/chronic-illness-parallel-calendars-patient-peer-networks-and-life-course-rerouting-china-1989-2026.zh-CN.md`
- appointment/access execution remains covered separately by `HOSPITAL_APPOINTMENT_PORTALS_PHONE_WEB_IDENTITY_AND_VISIT_STATE_GAPS_1999_2011.md`

This note stays within the repository's Chinese-Web scope. It does **not** attempt a global history of online patient communities, and it does not treat present-day medical platforms as old-Web artifacts.

---

## 1. Research question

Chinese patient forums make a special old-Web archaeology problem because they sit at the intersection of several states that must not be collapsed:

```text
real person
→ real medical condition or test result
→ person's own understanding of that condition
→ online account / pseudonym
→ post or thread
→ replies / private contacts / offline meetings
→ advice or rights information encountered
→ action taken offline
→ medical / employment / education / relationship outcome
→ later edits, deletion, account loss, site migration
→ archive capture
```

A surviving page may preserve only one small point in this chain.

Hard rules:

- `forum post ≠ clinical diagnosis`
- `self-reported test result ≠ medical record`
- `registered account ≠ unique patient`
- `username ≠ stable real-world identity`
- `pseudonym ≠ anonymity`
- `reply ≠ medically correct advice`
- `reply visible ≠ reply read`
- `reply read ≠ advice followed`
- `support contact ≠ treatment delivered`
- `rights information encountered ≠ discrimination resolved`
- `public disclosure in one context ≠ consent to cross-context identification`
- `archive capture ≠ permission to republish sensitive health information`

The key archaeological question is therefore not merely “what did patients say online?” It is:

> **What platform state is actually preserved, what real-world state can it support, and where must the reconstruction stop?**

---

## 2. Why “肝胆相照” is a useful bounded case family

Contemporaneous reporting gives unusually strong evidence that the Chinese old Web could become infrastructure for people who shared a stigmatized health status.

A 2003 *Shanghai Youth Daily* / Sina report already identified the “肝胆相照” forum's rights board and described a board moderator helping organize legal support around what contemporary media called China's first hepatitis-B employment-discrimination case.[1]

A separate 2003 contemporaneous Sina report preserved a more detailed platform-use scene: a constitutional-review proposal was posted to the forum, attracted more than 200 replies, was iteratively revised through member feedback, and developed into a signature effort. The same report stated that the rights board was then producing more than 1,000 posts per day and that some users spent many hours on the forum.[2]

A 2005 *羊城晚报* report described the forum as having about 130,000 members and showed that online ties also moved offline: members in Guangzhou organized a birthday gathering and used the shared label “战友”. It also recorded a sequence of forum-related rights actions from 2003 onward.[3]

These are strong **contemporaneous secondary observations** of platform existence, social practice, and some observable activities. They do **not** establish the original DOM, database schema, unique-human user count, or private-message state.

### Founding-date caution

Later accounts use different milestones when they say when “肝胆相照” began. Some refer to the broader forum around 2001; others date the dedicated rights board to 2003-04-05. Do not force these into one date without an original administrative or site artifact.

Record separately:

```text
community/site emergence = candidate date
rights-board creation = separate candidate date
registered member count at later dates = separate observation
```

## 3. Observable forum state versus real health state

A patient forum often contains sentences of the form:

```text
“我的两对半是……”
“医生说……”
“单位体检查出……”
“我已经吃了某药……”
```

From an archive perspective, these are **user assertions**.

Allowed reconstruction:

```text
account U posted text T at visible forum time V
```

Potentially allowed with careful phrasing:

```text
U self-reported that a test / diagnosis / treatment occurred
```

Not allowed without independent evidence:

```text
U clinically had diagnosis D
D had severity S
treatment X was medically appropriate
U's employer actually possessed the exact medical record described
```

This distinction is especially important because old medical forums are attractive to later researchers precisely because clinical records are private and unavailable. The public forum cannot be used as a substitute medical chart.

---

## 4. 2003: a thread could become collaborative document production

The 2003 report on the forum's constitutional-review proposal is useful because it reveals more than “discussion existed.” It shows a state chain:

```text
initial post
→ replies
→ critique / proposed edits
→ revised text
→ call for signatures
→ collection of identifying information from willing signers
→ document delivery outside the platform
```

This is a case of **thread-to-document transformation**.

It requires several boundaries:

- `thread exists ≠ every reader endorsed it`
- `reply count ≠ unique participant count`
- `signature intent ≠ valid submitted signature`
- `submitted identity ≠ public forum identity`
- `forum drafting ≠ government receipt`
- `government receipt ≠ policy change caused by that single thread`

The old Web here is not merely a publication layer. It can be a workspace that coordinates a later physical or institutional action.

## 5. 2003–2006: online weak ties could become offline support

Contemporaneous reports in 2003, 2005, and 2006 describe forum users traveling to support employment-discrimination litigation, meeting other forum users offline, and organizing collective letters or signatures.[1][3][4]

This creates an important state boundary:

```text
online co-membership
≠ direct interpersonal relationship
≠ offline meeting
≠ durable support tie
```

A user might read anonymously, post once, become an active moderator, join an offline gathering, or later disappear entirely. These are different relationship states.

For a reconstructed forum artifact, record separately where evidence permits:

- visible username;
- account registration metadata;
- post count;
- thread participation;
- reply network;
- claimed location;
- invitation to offline activity;
- evidence that an offline meeting actually occurred;
- later continuity of the tie.

Do not infer the latter states from the former.

## 6. 2007–2010: legal rules, forum knowledge, and the health-identity boundary

Government documents in 2007 began restricting routine hepatitis-B testing in general employment examinations and emphasized privacy protection. In 2010, national rules further prohibited ordinary education and employment examinations from requiring hepatitis-B testing or asking applicants to provide such reports, except for specifically approved occupational cases.[5][6]

The platform state did not become irrelevant when the legal state changed.

A 2008 *人民日报* report preserved by CCTV described a forum moderator whose mobile number, originally visible through an online thank-you letter, was linked back to his health status by classmates and reposted into a class forum. The report says he was then removed from a QQ group and deleted by some contacts. Another litigant insisted on anonymity because he feared that future employers and acquaintances could identify his condition.[7]

For old-Web archaeology this produces a crucial rule:

> **A pseudonymous health community can be socially safe only while cross-context linkage remains difficult.**

State chain:

```text
health forum pseudonym
→ public post / contact detail
→ searchable or forwarded page
→ phone / school / QQ / class-forum linkage
→ real-person inference
→ disclosure in a different social context
→ offline consequence
```

This is **cross-context identity linkage**, not merely “a privacy setting.”

Hard boundaries:

- `phone number visible ≠ real identity proven`
- `identity inferred ≠ inference correct`
- `class-forum repost exists ≠ everyone in class saw it`
- `QQ removal after disclosure ≠ disclosure was the sole cause unless contemporaneous testimony supports it`
- `forum pseudonym ≠ consent for researchers to identify the person decades later`

## 7. Search, indexing, and the accidental persistence of sensitive identity

Patient forums make search-engine archaeology unusually risky.

A page can remain discoverable after the user's original social context has ended. A search engine, a copied article, or a quoted username can connect fragments that were never intended to be read together.

This creates a three-layer distinction:

```text
publicly accessible at time t
≠ widely discoverable at time t
≠ ethically appropriate to re-identify at time t+20 years
```

For this repository:

- do not concatenate old usernames, phone numbers, school names, workplaces, and disease status to identify a private person;
- do not republish phone numbers or identifying medical details merely because an old page exposed them;
- do not use breach/leak datasets to reconstruct a patient's account;
- prefer already-public institutional or journalistic descriptions when the historical mechanism can be established without reproducing the person's sensitive details.

This is a **research stop condition**, not an evidence inconvenience.

## 8. Forum counts are platform counts, not population counts

Contemporaneous media gave large membership or activity figures for “肝胆相照”: around 130,000 members in 2005, and later accounts gave still larger registration counts.[3]

Do not convert these into:

- 130,000 unique hepatitis-B carriers;
- 130,000 active users;
- a percentage of all Chinese carriers;
- a measure of forum reach across regions or classes.

Potential distortions include:

- duplicate accounts;
- inactive registrations;
- family members / supporters / doctors;
- users who never posted;
- users sharing terminals or accounts;
- deleted or banned accounts;
- counting rules changing between software versions.

Minimum wording:

> contemporaneous reporting said the forum had approximately N registered members/accounts at date D.

Not:

> N patients used the forum.

## 9. Lurkers and the encounter gap

The most historically consequential forum use may leave no public post at all.

Possible state:

```text
search disease term
→ land on thread
→ read for 20 minutes
→ never register
→ change what to ask a doctor / employer / partner
→ never return
```

Archive evidence may retain the thread but nothing about that encounter.

Therefore:

`thread captured ≠ known readership`

and

`no account/post ≠ no influence`.

This is the **lurker / encounter-history gap**.

It matters especially for health because many users may deliberately avoid posting due to stigma or privacy risk.

## 10. Replies have at least four different evidence roles

A reply to a health thread can be:

1. emotional support;
2. practical everyday experience;
3. institutional / rights information;
4. medical or quasi-medical advice.

These must not be treated identically.

For archaeological annotation, add a `reply_role` where possible:

```text
support
experience
institutional-procedure
medical-claim
commercial-promotion
moderation
unknown
```

And add `verification_state`:

```text
unverified user claim
cross-confirmed by contemporaneous official source
contradicted by official source
medical claim outside repository competence
unknown
```

A highly upvoted or frequently repeated treatment claim is still not clinical evidence.

---

## 11. Moderation is part of the historical object

Patient communities often need moderation against:

- commercial drug advertisements;
- miracle-cure claims;
- repetitive panic posts;
- harassment;
- doxxing;
- misinformation;
- conflict over rights strategy.

But a surviving thread does not reveal all removed material.

State chain:

```text
submitted post
→ accepted / queued / rejected
→ visible
→ edited / moved / locked
→ deleted / soft-deleted
→ quoted elsewhere
→ captured by archive
```

Thus:

`not present in current/captured forum ≠ never posted`.

A future M1 case should attempt to recover contemporaneous forum rules, moderator notices, board descriptions, and software behavior before interpreting surviving discourse as the complete range of what users said.

---

## 12. Public support shell / private health-state asymmetry

The public Web is structurally more likely to preserve:

- forum homepage;
- board names;
- FAQ pages;
- public threads;
- moderator announcements;
- media reports about the community;
- rights documents.

It is structurally unlikely — and often ethically undesirable — to preserve:

- private messages;
- medical scans;
- real-name registration data;
- IP logs;
- phone numbers;
- offline meeting rosters;
- clinic records;
- employer examination files;
- later treatment outcome.

Call this **public-support-shell / private-health-state asymmetry**.

A future researcher must not “fill” the private half with speculation.

---

## 13. Crisis-post survival bias

Health communities create a specific source bias.

Threads most likely to attract replies, links, screenshots, media quotation, or later memory are often:

- a fresh diagnosis;
- a frightening test result;
- a treatment conflict;
- discrimination;
- a lawsuit;
- a desperate request for help.

Routine years of medication, work, family life, and uneventful checkups produce fewer archival events.

Therefore:

> **A forum archive can systematically make chronic life look more crisis-dense than it was.**

This is **crisis-post survival bias**.

Countermeasure: combine forum artifacts with contemporaneous surveys, life-history interviews, institutional statistics, and evidence of routine long-term survival rather than sampling only the most linked threads.

## 14. Board taxonomy can reveal how the platform modeled life

If a verified historical forum index is recovered, board names themselves can be evidence.

For example, separate boards for:

- treatment;
- testing;
- employment / rights;
- marriage / family;
- local meetups;
- emotional support;

would show that the platform modeled the condition as more than a medical problem.

But this claim requires a verified historical board index.

A modern mirror, later screenshot, or reporter's description may provide a **candidate board taxonomy**, not automatically the taxonomy at an earlier date.

Record:

```text
board label
capture datetime
original URL
software/version clues
board id if visible
access requirements
whether posting required login
whether reading required login
moderator names as displayed, without re-identification
```

## 15. Archive reconstruction requirements for a future M1 case

A complete patient-forum case should seek, at minimum:

### Artifact identity

- original URL;
- capture timestamp;
- archive provider;
- HTTP status;
- encoding / charset;
- title and board path;
- forum-software clues;
- visible account/pseudonym state.

### Page structure

- post id / thread id if visible;
- timestamps;
- pagination;
- quoting behavior;
- edit markers;
- signatures/avatars;
- login-dependent controls;
- moderation markers;
- deleted-post representation.

### State boundaries

- public board versus private message;
- guest read versus registered read;
- registered account versus verified identity;
- post timestamp versus server persistence time;
- view/reply counters versus unique humans;
- current page versus archived historical body.

### Environment

- charset handling (GB2312 / GBK / UTF-8 where actually evidenced);
- broken image / CSS / JS dependencies;
- cookie/session requirements;
- whether forms in replay are dead historical shells;
- whether search depends on unavailable backend state.

## 16. Current evidence status and negative result

This run obtained strong contemporaneous reportage from 2003, 2005, 2006, and 2008 describing forum practices, user interaction, offline coordination, and cross-context health disclosure.[1][2][3][4][7]

It did **not** obtain and open a historical 2001–2010 “肝胆相照” page capture that satisfies the repository's M1 standard with verified:

- original URL;
- capture datetime;
- HTTP response;
- original charset;
- DOM / page body;
- forum software/version;
- cookie/session behavior;
- board/thread identifiers;
- historical post persistence semantics.

Therefore:

- no M1 checkbox should be changed;
- current pages, media quotations, search snippets, and candidate Wayback locators must remain separate evidence classes;
- this note is a **state-gap model and source map**, not a reconstructed forum artifact.

---

## 17. What this changes in the old-Web model

The Chinese old Web did not only make information “available.” In a patient forum it could make a previously isolated **life state** addressable.

A user might go from:

```text
I have a condition but know nobody like me
```

to:

```text
I can find hundreds of prior questions, pseudonymous peers, procedural knowledge,
rights strategies, and sometimes offline contacts.
```

But the same infrastructure also made a second transition possible:

```text
health state disclosed in one bounded community
→ searchable / linkable fragment
→ identity linkage in school, work, QQ, or another forum
```

The historically correct conclusion is therefore double-sided:

> **patient forums expanded peer-state discoverability while creating new persistence and context-collapse risks around extremely sensitive identity.**

That dual state should remain visible in any future reconstruction.
