# Network photo albums, memory custody, shutdown, and private archive gaps (2003–2019)

## Scope

This note stays within the repository's Chinese-old-Web scope by treating **2003–2015 network photo albums as the historical object**. The 2019 NetEase Album shutdown is used only as **afterlife evidence** showing what happened to an in-scope service and its accumulated user data; it does not move the repository's main scope to 2019.

The research question is archaeological rather than nostalgic:

> When ordinary users used a Web photo service as both a sharing surface and long-term storage, what parts of that system can still be demonstrated after the service disappears?

The answer is structurally uneven. Public announcements, press reports, old URL strings and forum links may survive. The private or semi-private albums whose loss mattered to users may be exactly the artifacts least likely to survive in a public archive and least appropriate to re-publish.

---

## 1. Research units: do not collapse these objects

For this topic the following must remain separate:

- **service/platform**: NetEase Album, China Yahoo Space/Album;
- **host**: e.g. `photo.163.com`;
- **account**: a user's NetEase/Yahoo identity;
- **album/directory**: a named collection under an account;
- **photo binary**: JPEG/GIF/etc.;
- **album shell**: HTML/frames/script/UI surrounding photos;
- **public share URL**;
- **authenticated/private view**;
- **shutdown notice**;
- **migration/download tool**;
- **press report quoting users or platform staff**;
- **archive capture**.

A surviving shutdown article is not a surviving album. A surviving album URL string is not a verified historical capture. A user saying photos were lost is evidence of perceived/experienced loss, not an independently audited file count.

## 2. Evidence found this run

### 2.1 2003 user-facing description of NetEase Album

A Hewlett Packard Enterprise community post dated 2003-10-31 points to:

```text
http://photo.163.com/
```

and describes the service to other users as offering:

- no-capacity-limit storage during the promotion framing;
- single and batch upload;
- directory-level batch download;
- directory/category sharing with friends;
- free registration through a NetEase Passport.

This is **B-grade contemporaneous user/community evidence**, not a verified NetEase original product page. It is still useful because it records how the product was presented and understood by a user in 2003.

Source:
- https://community.hpe.com/t5/%E7%81%8C%E6%B0%B4%E5%8C%BA/%E5%88%86%E4%BA%AB%E6%88%91%E7%9A%84%E6%8A%80%E5%B7%A7-%E7%BB%99%E5%A4%A7%E5%AE%B6%E6%8E%A8%E8%8D%90%E4%B8%80%E4%B8%AA%E5%A5%BD%E4%B8%9C%E8%A5%BF-%E6%97%A0%E9%99%90%E9%87%8F%E7%9A%84%E7%BD%91%E6%98%93%E7%9B%B8%E5%86%8C/td-p/1587243

### 2.2 2005 URL patterns survive in unrelated forums

Contemporaneous forum pages preserve public NetEase Album URL strings such as:

```text
http://photo.163.com/openalbum.php?username=<user>
http://photo.163.com/openalbum.php?username=<user>&_dir=<directory>&page=<n>
```

Examples include sports/image-sharing threads and hobby forums in 2005. This is important because it demonstrates both a historical URL form and the way an album service was embedded into the wider Web: users posted album links in independent forums rather than keeping all interaction inside NetEase.

For privacy minimization, this note records the URL pattern rather than reproducing ordinary users' account names.

Sources:
- https://chinaspurs.com/t/topic/14106
- https://trow.cc/board/showtopic%3D2769%26view%3Dold

**What this proves:** URL pattern + public sharing practice existed in contemporaneous pages.

**What this does not prove:** the historical NetEase album HTML, DOM, charset, login state, image completeness, or browser behavior.

### 2.3 2007 service failure exposes storage dependence

A 2007-01-27 *Caijing Shibao* report carried by Sina describes a user who had opened a NetEase album in October 2006 because of the advertised unlimited space and batch upload, then placed all of his household's digital photos there. After the service became inaccessible, he did not know how much could be recovered.

The report also records user-created QQ rights groups and contemporaneous backup advice.

Source:
- https://tech.sina.com.cn/i/2007-01-27/13231356731.shtml

This is **B-grade contemporaneous media evidence with first-person testimony**. It is strong evidence that, for at least some users, the service had crossed from a presentation layer into primary or near-primary photo custody.

### 2.4 2007 dormant-account rule survives only through a user blog

A 2007-06-06 Blog园 post reproduces a notice attributed to the NetEase Album product team saying accounts inactive for more than three months would be frozen and photos in accounts inactive for more than five months would no longer be retained. The blogger adds that he had liked NetEase Album but had not updated it for a long time.

Source:
- https://www.cnblogs.com/JustinYoung/archive/2007/06/06/774259.html

This is **B-grade contemporaneous user-preserved notice evidence** unless/until an original NetEase notice or historical capture is verified. It should not be upgraded to A simply because the reproduced text looks official.

Archaeological significance: a long-term photo archive can become valuable precisely when it is no longer frequently opened. A platform rule based on account activity therefore may conflict with archival use.

### 2.5 2009 China Yahoo shutdown: public notice survives, private albums do not

A 2009-12-23 *China Youth Daily* report records that China Yahoo Space/Album had stopped on 2009-10-30. It says some users lost all four years of university photographs or all photographs since a child's birth; the shutdown notice had been placed in the help center, while many users learned of the closure only after they could no longer log in. Yahoo staff acknowledged that some users accessed the service only every month or even every year, and described people using the album like a multi-gigabyte mobile hard disk.

Source:
- https://news.sina.cn/sa/2009-12-23/detail-ikftpnny4229328.d.html

This is **B-grade contemporaneous reporting**, combining user statements, a company spokesperson's statements, and the reporter's reconstruction of the notice. It is not a historical capture of the China Yahoo album interface or help-center page.

### 2.6 2019 NetEase shutdown as afterlife evidence

NetEase Album launched within this repository's scope (2003) but shut down in 2019. Its shutdown is therefore useful as afterlife evidence for an old-Web system.

Two contemporary reports are especially useful:

- CCTV Finance / *National Business Daily*, 2019-05-23: a user who joined in 2008 to share class-reunion photos gradually forgot the service as newer social media appeared; he rediscovered the archive only after a friend mentioned the shutdown. Some files were already damaged or hard to download. The shutdown was postponed after users reported they had not finished saving photos.
- Red Star News, 2019-06-01: users described albums spanning a child's first six years, student life, relationships, childbirth, a deceased father, and photographs of a deceased childhood friend. Some users reported receiving notice too late or only discovering closure after the service no longer worked.

Sources:
- https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2019-05-23/1335086.html
- https://static.cdsb.com/micropub/Articles/201906/c0ca9c68aab6dd135ef96b184206d61d.html

These sources are **B-grade contemporary afterlife evidence**, not M1 historical captures of the 2003–2015 service.

## 3. New archive asymmetries

### 3.1 Public shutdown notice survival / private album disappearance

The closure itself is publicly documented. Ordinary albums are often private, semi-private, authenticated, or composed largely of binary image resources.

Therefore:

```text
shutdown-event visibility > ordinary-private-memory visibility
```

A future researcher may know exactly when a service closed while having almost no ethically reusable evidence of what an ordinary family album looked like in daily use.

### 3.2 URL-pattern survival / content non-survival

Independent forums preserve strings like:

```text
photo.163.com/openalbum.php?username=<user>&_dir=...
```

Even when the target no longer functions.

This is useful archaeology: a dead outgoing link can preserve interface topology and naming conventions. But it cannot be treated as preserved target content.

### 3.3 Album-shell survival / binary-resource loss

Even if an HTML album page is eventually found in an archive, photo binaries may not have been captured, may have been hosted at separate image hosts, or may require authentication/session state.

Therefore future cases must record separately:

- main HTML captured?;
- thumbnail resources captured?;
- original-resolution resources captured?;
- JS/CSS captured?;
- public vs authenticated state?;
- archive rewrites?

A page with broken thumbnails is evidence of a page shell, not evidence that the photographs themselves survived.

### 3.4 Account-auth dependency

A public forum link may expose a public album directory, while the user's management/download interface depends on an authenticated account. Shutdown therefore affects at least two different surfaces:

- public historical visibility;
- owner's ability to retrieve original files.

The latter can fail even if some public thumbnails or derived URLs remain discoverable elsewhere.

### 3.5 Active-use → dormant-archive transition

The 2008→2019 NetEase testimony demonstrates a useful afterlife state: a service can stop being a person's active social platform but continue holding old photos for a decade.

This produces an archaeological blind spot. Usage metrics that show declining activity do not necessarily mean the remaining data have low personal value.

### 3.6 Migration surface / preservation outcome gap

Before the 2019 shutdown, third parties offered migration tools, including tools that moved NetEase Album images into QQ Space or Baidu Netdisk.

Sources:
- https://www.ithome.com/0/421/306.htm
- https://news.mydrivers.com/1/624/624828.htm

A migration interface proves a migration route existed. It does **not** prove:

- every user saw the notice;
- every user could authenticate;
- every file was intact;
- private/public permission semantics were preserved;
- metadata/order/comments survived;
- the transfer finished before deadline;
- the destination platform offered permanent custody.

Thus:

```text
migration surface != preservation guarantee
```

## 4. A further methodological warning: the most archivally visible loss may be the least representative life

Media reports naturally select dramatic losses: years of university photographs, a child's birth, a dead parent, a dead friend. These are historically important because they reveal what users entrusted to the service.

But they are not a probability sample of all albums. Many albums contained wallpapers, sports images, public picture collections, product photographs or disposable sharing copies. Contemporaneous 2005 forum links demonstrate such non-family uses as well.

Therefore:

```text
archivally vivid testimony != prevalence estimate
```

The correct claim is that network albums **could** become long-term autobiographical infrastructure for some ordinary users, not that every user used them this way.

## 5. Overseas comparison: Yahoo Photos, 2007

Yahoo Photos provides a useful non-Chinese comparison. In 2007 Yahoo announced closure and offered users multiple migration destinations (including Flickr and third-party services), full-resolution download, and in some cases archive CD options. Contemporary reporting states that photos would be deleted and become inaccessible if users did not choose an option by the shutdown deadline.

Sources:
- TechCrunch, 2007-05-03: https://techcrunch.com/2007/05/03/breaking-yahoo-to-announce-closure-of-yahoo-photos-tomorrow/
- TechCrunch, 2007-06-14: https://techcrunch.com/2007/06/14/yahoo-photos-begins-closing-down/
- Computerworld via Macworld, 2007-08-23: https://www.macworld.com/article/187070/yahoophotos-2.html

Do not merge this event with **China Yahoo Space/Album's 2009 closure**. The products, dates, notification processes and legal/business context differ.

The cross-national mechanism is narrower: photo services can accumulate long-lived user data, and product shutdown transforms preservation into a time-bounded migration task.

## 6. Historical capture status in this slice

### M1 status: **NOT ACHIEVED**

This run did **not** verify a historical NetEase Album or China Yahoo album capture to the repository's METHOD minimum contract.

Searches attempted included:

- `photo.163.com` + Wayback/archive terms for 2003/2006/2007;
- `photo.163.com/openalbum.php` historical URL variants;
- China Yahoo album/space + 2009 archive terms;
- later references that appear to point at old NetEase Album archive locators.

Search results yielded contemporaneous pages that contain old URLs, but no historical capture was successfully opened and checked for all of:

- `original_url`;
- `capture_datetime`;
- replay/HTTP state;
- redirect/error/login state;
- historical Content-Type / charset where observable;
- DOM/frames/forms;
- JS/CSS/image subresources;
- second historical timepoint.

Accordingly this note does **not** claim:

- “Wayback did not save NetEase Album”;
- “the original 2003 page is unavailable everywhere”;
- any historical charset;
- any IE/ActiveX/Flash requirement;
- any exact DOM or frameset architecture.

Those remain unknown until a capture is actually opened and inspected.

## 7. Candidate technical clues for future artifact work

The following are **locators/patterns**, not verified captures:

```text
http://photo.163.com/
http://photo.163.com/openalbum.php?username=<user>
http://photo.163.com/openalbum.php?username=<user>&_dir=<directory>&page=<n>
```

Future work should search for:

1. a 2003–2005 homepage capture;
2. a contemporaneous public album capture;
3. a 2006–2007 album/help notice timepoint;
4. a second timepoint after UI/product changes;
5. image-host subdomains referenced by captured album HTML;
6. contemporary user instructions or screenshots that can constrain the interface when archive resources are incomplete.

Privacy rule: do not choose an ordinary person's intimate family album merely because it happens to be recoverable. Prefer public demonstration albums, institutional/product examples, or accounts whose content is clearly non-sensitive and research-necessary.

## 8. What is confirmed / probable / unknown

### Confirmed from contemporaneous evidence

- NetEase Album was publicly referenced at `photo.163.com` in 2003 user material.
- 2003 user material described batch upload/download and sharing as central functions.
- 2005 forum pages preserve `openalbum.php?username=...` public URL patterns.
- At least one 2006–2007 user reported putting all household digital photos into NetEase Album and experiencing prolonged access failure.
- A 2007 blog preserved a notice attributed to NetEase about inactivity-based freezing/deletion; original notice not yet verified.
- China Yahoo Space/Album shut down in 2009, and contemporaneous reporting records users losing long-term personal photo collections.
- NetEase Album's 2019 shutdown produced contemporary reports of users treating it as a decade-spanning archive.
- Migration/download routes existed before the 2019 shutdown.

### Probable / strongly suggested

- Network photo albums often occupied a hybrid role: public/semi-public sharing plus personal storage.
- As social attention shifted to newer platforms, old photo services could remain as low-activity, high-personal-value archives.
- Private/authenticated album content is likely to be systematically underrepresented in public Web archives relative to public shutdown pages and media reports.

### Unknown in this slice

- NetEase Album's exact 2003/2005 DOM and browser behavior.
- Historical charset and Content-Type of the target album pages.
- What share of user albums were public, private, password-protected, or authenticated-only at each timepoint.
- What proportion of image binaries survive independently of album HTML.
- How many users used the service as their only copy.
- Success rates of 2009/2019 user migration.
- Whether a suitable two-timepoint public demonstration album can satisfy M1 without creating privacy problems.
